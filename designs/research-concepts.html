What research-program sites do (read: RSI, Regeneron STS, Pioneer, Polygence, NHSJS)

  1. A dated deadline or status up top. RSI, STS, Pioneer and Polygence all put "apply by…" or "applications closed" before anything else.
  2. One fixed facts block. Pioneer gives every program the same Details box: dates, eligibility, format, cost. STS shows a dated timeline (application → Top 300 → finals).
  3. Papers as cards. NHSJS (the strongest listing) and Pioneer: title, student author, subject tag, date, abstract excerpt, PDF link. NHSJS groups them in subject rows.
  4. Proof = concrete numbers + named students. STS pairs a few hard numbers with finalist quotes and photos. Pioneer and Polygence lean on stat tiles.
  5. Mentors by credential. "All our mentors are professors" (Pioneer), "PhD-level, background-checked" (Polygence). Nobody shows mentor faces on the homepage, which is an opening for us.
  6. Avoid: adjective-only proof (RSI's page has no numbers, papers or outcomes); stat-stacked heroes and repeated testimonial blocks (Pioneer, Polygence); a bare paper index with no "how it works" or next step (NHSJS). Admission-rate stats are off-mission for us anyway.
Concept A

The Journal

The site is the publication. Light, editorial, a printed-journal masthead and a table of contents of student papers you can filter by field. The program sits below as "How to get published". Feels the most credible and scholarly; it needs real papers to look good, so it fits best after the first cohort.

Borrowed from NHSJS and Pioneer (paper cards: title, author, field tag, abstract, PDF), and unlike NHSJS it keeps "how to get published" on the same page.

Concept B

The Pathway

Program first. The navy hero matches the hub (same gradient, gold highlight), with the next cohort's dates and deadline right beside it. Then the three steps with weeks (Learn → Research → Publish), what you get, who can join, and mentors as faces. Works today, before any paper exists; papers get added later as a "Past work" strip.

Borrowed from Pioneer (facts block: dates, eligibility, format, cost), RSI / STS (deadline up front, weeks spelled out) and Polygence (mentor credentials), plus mentor faces, which none of them show.

Concept C

Question-first

Starts from curiosity, not the program: "What do you want to find out about your region?" Field chips and a wall of research questions: some answered (by a student, with a paper), some still open (gold, "take this one"). A "Fergana is the lab" section ties it straight to the mission: local questions nobody has studied yet. The most distinctive and youth-friendly; needs the team to curate a list of open questions.

Borrowed from NHSJS (browse by subject) and STS (finished projects as a showcase), with our own twist: the open-question board.
