# Turon Research — benchmark of similar programmes (2026-11-28)

Planned format: **1. Research School** (teach many) → **2. Competition** (they compete) → **3. Research Programme** (5–6 finalists write real papers with professors from top universities).

Turon Research is still not on the site (see CLAUDE.md). This file is only planning notes.

## Programmes compared

| Programme | Who runs it | Format | Cost | What Turon can take from it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RSI** (MIT, run by CEE) | Nonprofit + MIT | ~100 students; 1 week of classes, then 5 weeks of research with a mentor (faculty, postdocs, grad students), ends with a written paper and a talk | Free | Closest to phase 3. Teach first, then research, then present. Mentors don't have to be professors only. |
| **MIT PRIMES / PRIMES-USA** | MIT maths dept. | Year-long, after school, remote for PRIMES-USA. Reading phase → research → final paper and talk. Mentors mostly grad students and postdocs | Free | Remote mentoring works. A reading phase before research. A year-long timeline is realistic for a real paper. |
| **Pioneer Academics** | Company (US) | Online, professor-mentored; credit through Oberlin College; own journal accepts ~2% of papers after double-blind review | Paid | Says clearly it **doesn't guarantee publication**. Copy that honesty. |
| **Polygence, Lumiere** | Companies | 1:1 with PhD mentors, online | Paid (aid available) | The "pay-to-play" model that ProPublica criticised: some services present their own outlets as independent journals. Turon being free and merit-based is the opposite, and worth saying. |
| **Regeneron ISEF** | Society for Science | Tiered fairs: local → regional/national → international final (~1,700–2,000 finalists, 70+ countries) | Free to enter (fairs vary) | Model for phase 2. Judges look at the project and the talk, not a quiz. A good phase-2 winner could later go to an ISEF-affiliated fair. |
| **Journal of Emerging Investigators (JEI)** | Founded by Harvard grad students, nonprofit | Free, peer-reviewed journal for middle/high school research; review is part of the teaching | Free | Student-led precedent. A safe first venue for school-level papers; peer review as a learning step. |
| **IMO national teams** | National olympiad bodies | Many students → regional → national rounds → **team of 6** | Free | The "many → 6" funnel is already well known. Easy to explain to parents and schools. |

### In the region
- **UNESCO Youth as Researchers (Central Asia)** — launched Jan 2025, all five Central Asian countries incl. Uzbekistan. Possible partner or endorsement.
- **Hogwarts Club, New Uzbekistan University** — student-led research community; Research Camp 2026 for university students and school graduates, up to 6 per project team. Closest local peer; also student-led; same team size as phase 3.
- **ISSAI Summer Research Program, Nazarbayev University** — 6 weeks, AI, grades 9–12 and university; 2026 cohort from Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Armenia.
- **Zerde** (Kazakhstan) — national research-project competition for young people.

## What makes Turon's model different
1. **It teaches before it selects.** RSI and PRIMES pick students who already shine; Turon opens the door wide first. That matches the Turon mission: regional youth shouldn't need to leave Fergana to get this.
2. **Free and merit-based**, unlike the paid mentorship market.
3. **Student-led**, like JEI and the NewUU Hogwarts Club, with the same proven team size (5–6) as IMO teams and Hogwarts project teams.

## Lessons and risks
- **Don't promise publication.** Promise a finished paper and a submission; Pioneer and JEI both say clearly that publication isn't guaranteed. Avoid predatory or "pay to publish" journals.
- **Name professors only once they've confirmed** (same rule as "no invented stats"). RSI/PRIMES show postdocs and PhD students can be the day-to-day mentors with a professor supervising.
- **Judge phase 2 like a research fair:** proposal + short talk + Q&A, scored by a rubric, not a test.
- **Authorship rules up front:** who is first author, what the student must do themselves.
- **Timeline:** a real paper needs months (PRIMES runs a year; RSI's 5 weeks gives a report, not a journal paper). Plan phase 3 as ~6–9 months, remote.
- **Phase 1 needs to give value on its own** (certificate, skills) so the many who don't make the final still gain something.

## What this means for the logo
The brand story is the funnel: **many → compete → few → a paper**. Two new concepts draw it directly:
- **F · Three phases** — three shorter-and-shorter bars (school, competition, programme) ending in a teal dot (the paper). Recommended.
- **E · Bracket** — four dots merging into one, a knockout bracket for the competition.
